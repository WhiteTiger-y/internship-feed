Change the world. Love your job.
This Systems Engineering Intern role is within Kilby Labs, the central R&D organization of Texas Instruments (TI). The lab is chartered to develop novel and
innovative technologies and strategies for new business opportunities, drive next generation, differentiated technologies for existing businesses and recruit
top technical talent for TI.
Role
In this summer Systems Engineering intern role, you will explore AI-enabled control for robotic hands, with a focus on learning policies for reliable grasping
and adaptive grip. You will train and evaluate policies in simulation, test them on robotic hand hardware, and help close the gap between the two. What you will
do:
- Research and implement reinforcement learning (RL) and other learning-based policies for robotic grasping.
- Develop simulation environments to train and evaluate policies for multi-finger robotic hands.
- Use tactile and joint feedback to adapt grip to different objects and disturbances, such as slip.
- Benchmark learned policies against conventional control techniques.
Responsibilities
- Policy Learning: Design, train and tune control policies for grasping, grip force control and coordinated finger motion.
- Simulation: Build and calibrate physics-based models of robotic hands and contact to train and validate policies.
- Hardware Validation: Deploy policies on robotic hand prototypes, analyze test data, and resolve differences between simulation and hardware results.
- Prototyping: Work with team members on hardware setup, mechanism improvements and test fixtures.
- Communication: Document methods and results, share findings with the team, and contribute to patents and publications.
Qualifications
Minimum Requirements
- Currently pursuing PhD degree in Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Computer Science, Robotics or related field with a background in
reinforcement learning, robot learning or control
- Cumulative 3.0/4.0 GPA or higher
Preferred Qualifications
- Strong foundation in reinforcement learning, imitation learning or control theory.
- Experience with robotics physics simulators (e.g. MuJoCo, Isaac Lab, PyBullet).
- Proficiency in Python and deep learning frameworks (e.g. PyTorch).
- Background in mechanical design (e.g. robotic mechanisms, CAD, prototyping) is a strong plus.
- Hands-on experience with robot hardware, sensors or real-time experiments.
- Creative thinker with hands-on problem solving and data-driven experimentation skills.
- Works well with others and can brainstorm potential solutions together.
