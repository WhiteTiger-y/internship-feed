At Toyota Research Institute (TRI), we’re on a mission to improve the quality of human life. We’re developing new tools and capabilities to amplify the human
experience. To lead this transformative shift in mobility, we’ve built a world-class team advancing the state of the art in AI, robotics, driving, and material
sciences.
This is a paid 12-week internship opportunity and is a hybrid, in-office role.
Here’s a glimpse into the Internship experience from some of our TRI interns!
Responsibilities
- Design and develop prototypes that enable embodied AI agents to support people collaborating on physical tasks.
- Leverage multimodal AI to understand human activities, task progress, and collaborative interactions.
- Develop and evaluate interaction strategies that enable embodied AI agents to adapt their assistance to different contexts and collaborative situations.
- Integrate AI models and interaction logic with robotic hardware to enable real-time human-robot interaction.
- Design and conduct user studies to investigate how AI assistance influences human collaboration, task performance, and user experience.
- Collect and analyze quantitative and qualitative data to evaluate system effectiveness and identify opportunities for improvement.
- Collaborate with a multidisciplinary team of research scientists and engineers across Toyota Research Institute.
- Communicate research findings through presentations and written reports, including potential submissions to leading international research venues.
Qualifications
- Currently pursuing a Ph.D. in Computer Science, Human-Computer Interaction, Robotics, Machine Learning, Cognitive Science, or a related field.
- Experience developing and prototyping interactive or embodied AI systems, such as interactive robots, multimodal assistants, or conversational agents.
- Familiarity with human-robot interaction (HRI), human-AI interaction, or human behavior modeling.
- Experience with experimental design, user studies, and quantitative or qualitative data analysis.
- Ability to work independently, iterate quickly, and communicate research findings clearly.
