What You’ll Learn
- Project Overview: In memory-centric AI accelerators, many memory devices each process data in place and only exchange small partial results, which are then
merged. As LLM attention evolves from grouped-query attention (GQA) to multi-head latent attention (MLA) and sparse top-k attention, the work done locally on
each device shrinks while cross-device merge and reduction increasingly dominate decode latency. Using the team’s in-house performance model and simulator, you
will explore the design space of merge/reduction architectures — where reduction happens, how devices are connected, and at what precision partial results are
exchanged — and propose an architecture that performs well across modern attention workloads.
- Skills You’ll Learn:
- Architecture-level performance modeling and design-space exploration for distributed, memory-centric AI accelerators
- How modern attention variants (GQA, MLA, sparse top-k attention) map onto distributed memory, and how they shift the balance between computation and
communication
- Hardware–software co-design of interconnect and reduction (collective) hardware for LLM inference
What You’ll Do
The AGI team at Samsung Semiconductor explores memory-centric system architectures for next-generation AI inference, where computation happens close to where
data lives. We co-design hardware and software to make large-scale LLM inference faster and more efficient, and our work informs Samsung’s future memory and
accelerator products. As an intern, you will own a research-driven architecture study end to end — from workload modeling to design-space exploration and an
architecture proposal — working closely with architects and performance-modeling engineers, and you will present your findings to the broader organization at
the end of the summer.
Location: Daily onsite presence at our San Jose headquarters in alignment with our Flexible Work polic
Reports to: Director
- Extend the team’s in-house performance model and simulator to capture modern attention workloads — GQA, MLA, and sparse top-k attention — during LLM decode.
- Analyze how each attention variant changes the balance between local computation and cross-device merge/reduction traffic, and pinpoint when merge becomes the
bottleneck.
- Explore the merge/reduction design space, including where reduction happens (centralized, in-network, or hierarchical), interconnect topology, link bandwidth
and latency, and partial-result precision.
- Characterize token-selection patterns of sparse attention models and design distributed top-k selection and data-placement strategies that minimize
communication and load imbalance.
- Estimate first-order area and power costs of proposed reduction hardware and weigh them against performance gains.
- Validate the numerical accuracy of reduced-precision merging against full-precision reference implementations.
- Collaborate weekly with your mentor and architecture engineers, and share your results through team meetings, a final presentation, and a technical report
that inform future accelerator architecture and potential publications or patents.
- Complete other responsibilities as assigned.
