Job Description
The intern will conduct hands-on research at the intersection of machine learning, statistics, and critical energy infrastructure security. The position focuses
on enhancing the reliability, transparency, and operator-readiness of AI-driven cybersecurity tools used to secure power grid substations and physical assets,
with a primary focus on explainability and robust uncertainty bounds.
- Explainable AI (XAI): Design and implement XAI methods (e.g., attribution maps, rule extraction, root-cause analyses) to make complex ML models transparent
and auditable for control-room operators and system administrators.
- Confidence & Uncertainty Quantification: Apply statistical techniques and frameworks (e.g., conformal prediction, Bayesian neural networks, or ensemble
methods) to quantify model confidence and measure predictive uncertainty in real-time.
- Grid Dataset Analysis: Analyze cyber-physical power system security datasets (such as 9-Bus system baseline and cyber-physical anomaly runs) to extract
feature patterns and validate anomaly detection model behavior.
- Model Safety & Robustness: Explore machine learning safety techniques, including model steering, to ensure grid security models behave predictably under
distribution shifts or input corruptions.
- Visualization & Alert Design: Support research into visualization methods and alert design that present explainability and confidence metrics clearly to
operators, assisting with alert prioritization.
- Collaboration & Reporting: Partner with cybersecurity researchers and AI development teams to integrate explainability modules, document results, and compile
guidelines for deploying reliable AI models.
