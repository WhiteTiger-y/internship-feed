Robotics & Software Engineering Intern
What are we trying to solve?
Openshelf is building an open-source system to automate physical inventory, combining hardware and software to handle storage, retrieval, and real-time
tracking.
- The problem: Managing physical inventory is messy. Manual processes are slow, error-prone, and don’t scale. It’s hard to know what you have, where it is, or
how much space it’s taking up.
- What we’re doing: We build compact automated storage and retrieval systems that capture weight, dimensions, and location data every time something moves.
We’re now adding robotic automation on top: cherry-picking, tube decapping, and liquid handling, so labs can store, open, sample, and return samples without
manual handling. Everything is open so others can adapt, improve, and deploy it, not just warehouses with big budgets.
Our stack runs Python on the backend (FastAPI, hardware orchestration, event-driven robot control) and a SvelteKit frontend, all containerized with Docker and
deployed on Raspberry Pi–based hardware. Mechanical design happens in Onshape.
What you’ll be doing
- Writing and debugging Python that controls real hardware: motors, motion axes, grippers, scales, barcode scanners, and sensors, over serial, Modbus, and GPIO.
- Helping bring up and test new robotic modules, from first motion to reliable operation.
- Supporting mechanical prototyping and testing: CAD changes in Onshape, 3D-printed fixtures, and tracking parts on the BOM.
- Building internal tools and dashboards to monitor, configure, and debug devices in our lab and in the field.
- Using AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, or similar) daily to move faster. We do, and we expect you to.
- Joining code review, design discussions (API structure, data modeling, error handling, state management), and deployments to real devices.
- Owning small projects end to end under the guidance of senior engineers, whether that’s a new sensor integration, a reliability fix, or a test rig.
