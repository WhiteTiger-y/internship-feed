The undergraduate research intern in HED and IFE project supports a research effort to develop a laser-driven alpha-particle source for investigating charged-
particle transport and energy deposition in high-energy-density plasmas. The intern will work with a multidisciplinary research team to model the transport and
energy selection of laser-driven alpha particles. Using Monte Carlo particle-transport simulations, the intern will investigate how particle energy, energy
spread, divergence, source size, and selector geometry affect energy resolution and transmission efficiency. The student will contribute to the development and
optimization of an advanced alpha-particle selector and help establish design requirements for future experiments. In addition, the student will have an
opportunity to participate in a hands-on experiment at a world-class petawatt laser facility. This work is relevant to inertial fusion energy, plasma physics,
particle-beam transport, and diagnostic development.
A U.S. Department of Energy National Laboratory managed by Princeton University, the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory (PPPL) is tackling the world’s toughest
science and technology challenges using plasma, the fourth state of matter. With more than 75 years of history, PPPL is a leader in the science and engineering
behind the development of fusion energy, a potentially limitless energy source. PPPL is also using its expertise to advance research in the areas of
microelectronics, quantum sensors and devices, and sustainability sciences. Whether it be through science, engineering, technology or professional services,
every team member has an opportunity to contribute to our mission and vision. Come join us!
Responsibilities
The key responsibilities for this position will be:
- Conduct Monte Carlo simulations of alpha particle transport through magnetic energy- selection systems
- Model the effects of particle energy, energy spread, divergence, source size, and charge state on selector performance.
- Evaluate key performance parameters, including energy resolution, transmission efficiency, particle losses, and temporal dispersion.
- Participate in a relevant experiment on alpha particle generation and selection at international laser user facility.
- Analyze simulation results and compare them with analytical estimates and available experimental data
- Support the development and optimization of an advanced alpha particle selector design.
- Perform parameter scans to determine how selector geometry and field configuration affect particle trajectories and energy selection
- Participate in regular research meetings and collaborate with members of the experimental and computational teams.
- Present research progress and results through technical presentations and a written final report.
