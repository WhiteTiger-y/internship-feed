The Position
2027 Summer Intern - AI for Drug Discovery
Department Summary
The Foundation Models team is seeking exceptional graduate interns with strong research experience in machine learning (ML) and a passion for applying advanced
algorithms to scientific discovery. Our team develops state-of-the-art generative models, autonomous scientific agents, and foundational ML methods to
accelerate drug design.
Autonomous agents increasingly run long scientific workflows in which they must decide, round after round, whether to continue a promising strategy, explore a
new one, stop an unproductive experiment, or revisit an earlier hypothesis. Yet we know little about whether these agents learn from experimental evidence the
way a good scientist would. This internship will study that question rigorously, using real multi-round drug discovery campaigns.
The intern will test whether agents make decisions for the right scientific reasons: whether they learn across rounds, distinguish strong evidence from weak or
confounded evidence, and account for epistemic uncertainty from limited knowledge and aleatoric uncertainty from inherent outcome variability. They will also
study how different representations of prior experience (raw history, compaction, structured summaries, self-reflection) shape this behavior. A central open
question is which failures can be addressed through how evidence is presented to the agent, and which require changing the model itself through post-training,
and how to tell the two apart in a principled way.
Intern candidates should have a strong interest in LLM agents, evaluation methodology, decision-making under uncertainty, and post-training. The ideal intern is
comfortable conducting independent research, rapidly prototyping ideas, and collaborating with multidisciplinary scientists.
This internship position is located in South San Francisco, on-site.
The Opportunity
- Design and build a benchmark of real and controlled counterfactual decision points from multi-round scientific design campaigns
- Develop quantitative metrics for decision quality under varying evidence strength, uncertainty, and cost
- Characterize recurring agent failure modes and develop a principled method for deciding whether each is best addressed through harness or context changes or
through post-training
- Explore post-training (e.g., supervised fine-tuning on historical trajectories and counterfactual examples) of smaller specialized models, and compare against
prompting and context-engineering baselines on held-out targets
- Develop well-documented, reproducible evaluation and training code and curated datasets, and present results for potential publication at internal or external
scientific conferences or workshops.
