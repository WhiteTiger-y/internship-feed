Overview
Research Internships at Microsoft provide a dynamic environment for research careers with a network of world-class research labs led by globally-recognized
scientists and engineers, who pursue innovation in a range of scientific and technical disciplines to help solve complex challenges in diverse fields, including
computing, healthcare, economics, and the environment.
Researchers in quantum applications are designing the algorithms, software, and workflows that will make fault-tolerant quantum computers useful for solving
real-world problems in chemistry, materials science, and physics.
Microsoft's deep investments in the field are advancing the state of the art in quantum algorithms, resource estimation, and hybrid quantum-classical workflows,
with applications designed alongside the hardware that will run them. In addition, the company's researchers take a rigorous, realistic view of where quantum
computers will outperform classical methods and what resources each application will require. The research, tools, and software that result from this investment
are built into the Microsoft Quantum Development Kit (QDK) and shared with the broader scientific community through open-source releases such as QDK/Chemistry
to accelerate discovery and help solve some of humankind's most challenging problems.
Responsibilities
Research Interns put inquiry and theory into practice. Alongside fellow doctoral candidates and some of the world’s best researchers, Research Interns learn,
collaborate, and network for life. Research Interns not only advance their own careers, but they also contribute to exciting research and development strides.
During the 12-week internship, Research Interns are paired with mentors and expected to collaborate with other Research Interns and researchers, present
findings, and contribute to the vibrant life of the community. Research internships are available in all areas of research, and are offered year-round, though
they typically begin in the summer.
The Research Intern will explore chemistry and physics applications for early fault-tolerant quantum computers, contributing to the development and evaluation
of algorithms and workflows that leverage emerging quantum capabilities, in combination with machine learning and/or AI. Potential research directions include:
- Quantum algorithms and simulation: Develop and analyze algorithms for quantum dynamics and complex quantum systems, including Hamiltonian simulation, and
study their error and computational resource requirements.
- Quantum computing for chemistry and materials: Develop computational methods, including downfolding techniques, that map complex chemical and materials
problems onto efficient representations suitable for quantum simulation.
- Machine learning and quantum systems: Investigate how quantum computing and machine learning can be used together to simulate complex quantum systems, this
includes exploring the limits of neural networks in capturing and learning many-body quantum complexity, to understand where quantum computers provide a true
advantage.
Working closely with researchers and engineers, the Research Intern will develop new algorithmic techniques and workflows, perform theoretical and numerical
analysis, and assess their potential for scientifically meaningful applications on fault tolerant quantum computers.
