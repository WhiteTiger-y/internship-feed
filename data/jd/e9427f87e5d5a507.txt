Future AR and VR devices occupy ever-smaller, glasses-like form factors and thus require ever-tighter power budgets. At the same time, we require higher-quality
passthrough rendering and scene understanding. In particular, depth estimation, which reconstructs scene geometry in real time from sensor signals, is one of
the biggest power consumers in AR and VR devices. However, most existing work targets datacenter GPUs, or at best mobile silicon with an order of magnitude more
power headroom than what glasses form factors allow.Reality Labs is Meta's innovation engine for next-generation AR/VR and wearable technology and XR Graphics
is involved in building the next-generation imaging and reconstruction stack that closes this gap. We are looking for a Research Scientist Intern to work on
low-power real-time reconstruction of scene geometry for AR/VR. Today, VR passthrough, hand interaction, and environment understanding run on separate
pipelines, each tuned for different accuracy, latency and power target. We are interested in deduplicating the compute (and thus reducing power):- What is the
right depth estimation and processing strategy, from the ground up, for a glasses power budget?- Can we train a single lightweight scene representation that can
serve passthrough, hand occlusions, and background occlusions simultaneously?These are open questions, and you would help define the approach. This is a
research internship. You will own a genuinely open problem, choose the methods, build the evaluation, and present your results, with support for publication
where the work warrants it.
Research Scientist Intern, Low-Power Scene Reconstruction for AR/VR Responsibilities:
- Plan and execute research to advance the state of the art in low-power, real-time scene reconstruction for AR/VR — spanning depth estimation, occlusion, and
geometric scene understanding.
- Explore the extent to which a shared, learned scene representation can serve multiple downstream consumers at different latency and accuracy points.
- Define evaluation methodology and run experiments measuring geometric accuracy, temporal stability, rendering distortion, latency, and compute and power cost
on real multi-sensor device captures.
- Collaborate with researchers and engineers across the passthrough, scene understanding, and on-device compute teams to assess feasibility within headset and
glasses power envelopes.
- Document and present findings, and contribute to publications and patents where the results warrant it.
