The InterDigital AI Lab is seeking exceptional Ph.D. students for research internships in machine learning, physical AI, robotics, and distributed vision-
language-action models.
Our research investigates how models and intelligent systems can learn, communicate, and operate together across architectures, modalities, tasks, and devices
through shared or interoperable representations. A major focus is physical AI: how intelligent machines (robots, drones, and autonomous vehicles) can perceive,
reason, and act in the real world when their models run across devices, the edge, and the cloud, and must share limited bandwidth, compute, and power.
What You Will Do
- Formulate an original AI/ML research problem in collaboration with one or more research mentors on our team.
- Develop and implement novel models, learning methods, or evaluation frameworks, and, where it makes sense, see them run on real robots over real networks.
- Design rigorous experiments and analyze model performance, behavior, and limitations.
- Present your findings and author publications at leading conferences in machine learning and AI.
Research Areas
Potential projects may fall within one or more of the following areas:
- Interoperable and modular AI: Enabling independently trained models, model components, and learned representations to be composed or exchange information.
- Multimodal and physical AI: Learning across vision, language, sensing, and action, including vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA)
models.
- Distributed vision-language-action systems: Splitting VLA models and VLMs across robot, edge, and cloud, including “fast/slow” designs where a lightweight on-
robot policy is guided by a large cloud-hosted model.
- Latency-robust perception and control: Helping robots perceive and act reliably when network delay, jitter, or bandwidth limits get in the way, using delay-
aware inference, world models, and future prediction.
- Efficient learning and representations: Developing semantic and information-efficient representations; compressing features, visual tokens, embeddings, and
action representations; dataset distillation and data-efficient learning algorithms; real-time world state modeling, motion modeling, action chunking, long-
context autoregressive diffusion, flow matching, and test-time training (TTT); and making VLA inference fast and frugal enough to run on the robot itself.
- Collaborative and multi-robot intelligence: Sharing perception and scene understanding across robots and sensors over wireless links, with benchmarks and sim-
to-real evaluation for networked embodied agents.
- Egocentric intelligence and memory: Understanding, organizing, and retrieving information from long-term multimodal and egocentric experience.
We are also open to original research ideas in machine learning that are aligned with the Lab’s broader research interests. Specific projects will be matched to
your expertise, interests, and research background.
