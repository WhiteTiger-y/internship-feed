About the Role:
As an Economics Research Intern on the Gusto Insights team, you’ll help us turn Gusto’s platform data into research on the small business economy, including
hiring, pay, entrepreneurship, and the future of work. Insights publishes widely cited public research, including the monthly Small Business Jobs Report. The
ideal candidate is a technically strong, curious economist who can turn messy data into a clear story and is passionate about helping small businesses thrive.
You’ll own at least one well-scoped, high-visibility project from our research roadmap and data-infrastructure backlog, taking it from raw data through analysis
to a shipped deliverable. You’ll work with mentorship from an established team and partner with colleagues across Communications, Marketing, and Data. You’ll
also use AI-assisted practices to accelerate your analysis and extend the reach of our research. The internship runs ~10–12 weeks in summer 2027.
This is a San Francisco-based role with expectations to be in office 2 days per week.
Here’s what you’ll do day-to-day:
- Own a Project End-to-End : Take a self-contained project from raw data through analysis to a finished, public-ready deliverable.
- Analytical Depth: Analyze large-scale, messy administrative data using Python and SQL. Apply econometric methods, including causal inference and regression
modeling, to separate signal from noise and make defensible claims.
- Build and Leverage AI: Use Claude Code and other agentic tools in your daily workflow for exploration, code, and drafting. Verify their output with a critical
eye.
- Communication: Turn analysis into clear charts and a crisp narrative for non-technical audiences. Our output is public-facing.
- Collaboration: Work with cross-functional partners to scope your project, get feedback, and deliver insights that matter.
- Economic Measurement : Sharpen the indicators we publish every month. You might build more detailed cuts of the Small Business Jobs Report by industry,
region, and company size, test whether Gusto data can predict BLS revisions, or define and validate a new economic metric from scratch.
- Data Infrastructure: Strengthen the foundation our research runs on. Turn one-off scripts into reproducible, documented pipelines, help move core workflows to
modern tools like dbt, and bring new datasets into our research environment so the team can answer questions it can’t today.
- Data Products & AI Interfaces: Build tools that put our research in more people’s hands. This could mean expanding our public economic data page, creating
interactive visualizations, or extending the AI agents and research assistants that let reporters, partners, and colleagues explore Gusto’s data and findings on
their own.
- Present: Share your findings with the Insights team and partners at the end of the internship.
Here’s what we’re looking for:
- Currently enrolled in a four year degree or master’s program with an expected graduation date of December 2027 or May 2028. Economics or applied economics is
the most likely fit, but public policy/economics, computational social science, and statistics or data science programs with strong economics training also
qualify.
- Strong Python skills and experience with SQL. You write clean, reproducible code.
- A foundation in applied economics and econometrics, including causal inference (diff-in-diff, matching, event studies), and a clear sense of the difference
between correlation and a defensible causal claim.
- Demonstrated fluency with LLMs and agentic coding tools (Claude Code, Cursor, or similar), and good judgment about when to verify their output.
- Genuine interest in the small business economy, labor markets, and entrepreneurship.
- Excellent written and visual communication skills.
- A proactive, resilient problem-solver who can independently structure ambiguous problems into actionable insights.
- Nice to have: version control experience and a willingness to build small tools or pipelines.
- Candidates must be authorized to work in the United States.
