About Georgian
Georgian is a Toronto-based venture capital firm that partners with high-growth technology companies across cybersecurity, AI software, robotics, defense. We
manage about $6B in assets and we have invested in more than 80 companies that we support with capital and technical capability.
Our in-house AI lab works directly with portfolio companies to provide a differentiated experience across the investment lifecycle, and drives the adoption of
AI for our internal operations.
About the Role
As an intern in Georgian’s AI Lab, you’ll work on the brain of the robot: the models it thinks with, the data it learns from, the simulators it practices in and
the systems that get all of that onto real machines. You’ll work alongside our scientists and engineers on research, portfolio projects and technical diligence,
and you’ll own a scoped project of your own. This is a software role. You won’t design hardware, but you’ll learn enough about the body to understand what it
will and won’t let the brain do.
Why This Role Exists
Physical AI is a core investment thesis area for us. Our portfolio includes Bedrock Robotics, Path Robotics, Vention, and other frontier robotics companies. We
think most of what separates these companies is the brain, not the body: how they collect and use data, the models that see and act, the world models and
simulators they train against and the tools that get a new behavior from the lab onto a fleet of machines. That’s the part of the stack you’ll work on as an
intern. You’ll help the AI Lab follow the latest in robot learning, run experiments and build tools, and support our work with portfolio companies and on
technical diligence. You’ll know how a learned policy behaves on a real robot and what a working sim-to-real pipeline looks like. And you’ll be able to tell a
system that’s really deployed from a good demo.
What You'll Own & Deliver
Support robot learning research. Help the team keep up with what’s new in VLAs, world models, sim-to-real and evaluation. Reproduce results from recent papers,
run experiments and ablation studies, and contribute to internal tech notes and write-ups.
Hands-on R&D. Train and evaluate policies in simulation under guidance, using imitation learning or reinforcement learning. Assist in building data pipelines,
evaluation setups and small tools that make experiments faster and easier to repeat.
Contribute to portfolio projects. Support AI Lab team members working with portfolio companies on robot-learning software. Assist with code implementation,
testing and documentation, and help track results the companies care about, like task success rate and how often a person has to step in.
Support technical diligence. Help the investment team assess physical AI companies by researching the technical landscape and preparing technical question
lists. Document what we find, including whether a company’s autonomy is learned or scripted and how it holds up beyond the demo.
Own a scoped project. Take one focused project from idea to result, such as a sim-to-real benchmark, a policy training pipeline or an evaluation of a new model,
and present it to the AI Lab and the wider Georgian team.
Communicate technical work. Turn what you learn into clear documentation, demos and short presentations for audiences with different technical backgrounds.
What We're Looking For
Ideal Attributes
Currently pursuing or recently completed a Master’s or PhD in computer science, robotics, electrical engineering or a related field, with a focus on machine
learning, reinforcement learning or robotics.
Hands-on experience training deep learning models in a modern framework (PyTorch, JAX or similar), with strong Python and clean, reproducible code.
Working knowledge of robot learning: imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and the basics of VLAs, world models and sim-to-real transfer.
Some exposure to robotics systems through coursework, research, a lab or a competition team, even if you haven’t deployed outside a lab
Comfortable with ambiguity and with asking good questions when you’re stuck; a clear writer and speaker
Nice-to-Haves
Experience with simulators (MuJoCo, Isaac Sim, ManiSkill or similar) or LeRobot
Time on real robot hardware, including research, hobby or competition projects
Basic C++ and ROS 2, enough to read an on-robot stack
Classical robotics background: motion planning, state estimation or control
Published work or open-source contributions in robot learning (CoRL, RSS, ICRA, LeRobot or similar)
Interest in industries like manufacturing, industrial automation or construction
Cultural attributes we love
Ambition and agency: You want to do big things and don’t wait to be told; you’re at your best with little structure and a lot of ownership
Curiosity: You chase problems to the root cause and keep asking “why,” even when the answer turns out to be a loose cable rather than the model
Team over ego: You make the people around you better, colleagues and founders alike
Plain talk: You can explain a hard technical thing briefly enough that a busy CEO gets it the first time
Builder: You’d rather ship a rough policy to a test cell today than a perfect one next quarter, and you know the difference between a demo and a deployment
Hiring Process
We don't hire like a MAG7 or scale up — in fact we rarely hire compared to them.
You shouldn't expect a cookie-cutter high-volume hiring process with us — but a more boutique one that allows you to see a lot of the organization before
joining us.
We build a thesis in what an individual brings to our very small team, versus simply matching qualifications:
30‑min intro (values & motivations)
60‑min technical interview (ML fundamentals & problem‑solving)
Project discussion with team members
