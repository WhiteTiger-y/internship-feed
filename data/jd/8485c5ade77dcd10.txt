Overview
Research Internships at Microsoft provide a dynamic environment for research careers with a network of world-class research labs led by globally-recognized
scientists and engineers, who pursue innovation in a range of scientific and technical disciplines to help solve complex challenges in diverse fields, including
computing, healthcare, economics, and the environment.
We are seeking Research Interns to make long-horizon Artificial Intelligence (AI) agents both more capable and more secure . Agents increasingly act
autonomously over long horizons and have access to tools, files, networks, and credentials. Two problems limit how far they can be trusted: they fail in ways
that aggregate benchmark scores hide, and they can be manipulated by adversarial inputs. This Research Internship offers two research directions, with Research
Interns focusing on one based on their expertise and interests:
- Failure-driven self-improvement: improve long-horizon agents by diagnosing failure modes in their trajectories and synthesizing targeted post-training data
that closes specific capability gaps.
- Agent security: develop technologies that make existing agentic systems more secure, including sandboxing, agent permissions, and defenses against novel agent
attacks.
Research Interns will contribute to one thrust, depending on their background. Expected outcomes include research prototypes, evaluation of pipelines, and
publications at top-tier venues.
Responsibilities
Research Interns put inquiry and theory into practice. Alongside fellow doctoral candidates and some of the world’s best researchers, Research Interns learn,
collaborate, and network for life. Research Interns not only advance their own careers, but they also contribute to exciting research and development strides.
During the 12-week internship, Research Interns are paired with mentors and expected to collaborate with other Research Interns and researchers, present
findings, and contribute to the vibrant life of the community. Research internships are available in all areas of research, and are offered year-round, though
they typically begin in the summer.
Additional Responsibilities
Project 1 — Self-Improving AI Agents
Develop and evaluate a research agent that uses post-training experiments to iteratively improve a target model through synthetic data.
- Task decomposition and capability diagnosis. Develop methods for the agent to infer the capabilities required by a target task and use evaluation results to
identify which capability gaps limit the model’s performance.
- Capability-targeted data synthesis. Build methods that translate hypotheses about capability gaps into training data, with control over skill coverage,
example difficulty, and data quality.
- Closed loop optimization. Build a self-improvement loop that measures the effects of each round of synthetic data. Use these results to let the agent revise
its capability hypotheses and adapt its data generation strategy across rounds.
Project 2 — Secure AI Agents
Develop and evaluate security mechanisms for existing AI agent systems.
- Sandboxing. Isolate agent execution and control access to files, networks, and credentials.
- Agent permissions. Develop least-privilege authorization, task-scoped permissions, and approval mechanisms for sensitive actions.
- Agent attacks and defenses. Study prompt injection, malicious tool outputs, memory poisoning, and cross-agent attacks in controlled environments. Prototype
defenses in an existing agent framework and evaluate their security effectiveness, impact on task performance, and usability.
Across both projects, Research Interns will collaborate with researchers to communicate findings to technical audiences and pursue publication at top-tier
venues
