NVIDIA is using the power of high-performance computing and AI to accelerate digital biology. Join our applied research team to study agent swarms: coordinated
teams of AI agents that plan, build, experiment, and check one another’s work. Working with a mentor, you will investigate how these systems can solve hard
problems in computational biology using NVIDIA’s accelerated computing platform. You will also use swarms as a daily research tool, rapidly turning ideas into
implementations, experiments, and useful scientific software. We are looking for exceptional researchers who combine deep computational foundations with
original thinking and careful scientific judgment. Expect a lively, collaborative environment with ambitious questions, short cycles from hypothesis to
experiment, and the fun of building things that work.
What You'll Be Doing
- Work with a mentor to formulate original research questions about how agent swarms can solve difficult computational biology problems, and design a focused
project to test them.
- Build and orchestrate agents that divide work, explore approaches in parallel, write and execute scientific code, exchange evidence, and critique or refine
one another’s solutions.
- Use agent swarms in your daily research workflow to rapidly implement algorithms, build scientific tools, and run experiments, taking responsibility for
correctness and scientific interpretation.
- Design controlled evaluations against single-agent systems and established computational methods; use ablation studies and comparable compute budgets to
measure solution quality, reliability, and time to solution.
- Collaborate with biologists, computational scientists, and engineers to connect experiments to meaningful biological questions; share reproducible code,
benchmarks, and findings through open-source releases and research publications where appropriate.
What We Need To See
- Pursuing an MS or PhD in computer science, computational biology, bioinformatics, chemistry, physics, mathematics, or a related quantitative field, or an
equivalent program area.
- Strong foundations in algorithms, data structures, numerical methods, and scientific computing, demonstrated through coursework, research, or substantial
projects.
- Proficiency in Python and experience implementing, testing, and debugging scientific software, including the ability to inspect and validate agent-generated
code and numerical results.
- Hands-on experience using and orchestrating multiple AI agents to solve technical problems, including task decomposition, tool use, parallel execution, and
verification of outputs.
- Evidence of research depth and originality through a project, thesis, paper, or software contribution: formulating a question, designing convincing
experiments, and critically interpreting findings and limitations.
Ways To Stand Out From The Crowd
- Experience turning scientific papers or mathematical descriptions into efficient implementations, including profiling, numerical validation, and optimization
in C/C++, CUDA, or other parallel computing environments.
- Experience applying computational methods to biological problems, such as sequence or structure analysis, molecular modeling, or genomics.
- Research on multi-agent systems for scientific discovery, with evidence of when coordination improves results over simpler approaches.
- Experience building agent systems with shared state, experiment tracking, automated tests, or recovery from failed runs, and an interest in making ambitious
research workflows reliable.
