Overview
Research Internships at Microsoft provide a dynamic environment for research careers with a network of world-class research labs led by globally-recognized
scientists and engineers, who pursue innovation in a range of scientific and technical disciplines to help solve complex challenges in diverse fields, including
computing, healthcare, economics, and the environment.
We are a team of experienced research scientists in Microsoft Research. Our mission is to co-design novel and intelligent software systems and hardware-aware
techniques to improve the efficiency, reliability, scalability, and trustworthiness of next-generation artificial intelligence (AI) systems. We build working
systems, engage with academia, publish scientific papers, publish software for the research community, and deploy cutting-edge technologies.
Are you excited by problems such as:
- Using AI agents to automate hardware experimentation, system management, and performance analysis.
- Reverse engineering the performance and security characteristics of modern graphics processing unit (GPU) servers and their memory systems.
- Understanding and overcoming performance bottlenecks in high-bandwidth GPU interconnects, such as NVLink and NVLink-C2C.
- Improving the efficiency of large language models (LLMs) serving by optimizing data movement and communication across central processing units (CPUs), GPUs,
and memory systems.
If you are, then we want to work with you!
Our team has deployed research to a multitude of Microsoft businesses, including Azure AI, Azure Hardware, Azure Networking, and Windows. We have also published
our research at the top systems and security academic conferences including ASPLOS, ISCA, USENIX Security, IEEE S&P, SOSP, ACM SIGCOMM, USENIX NSDI.
As part of a small, tight-knit team, your work will be central to our focus and attention throughout the summer and beyond. Find out more about the team at:
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/group/software-hardware-co-design-sharc/ . We practice open communication and respectful dialogue. We support multiple
social and career building opportunities in our internship program.
We seek Research Intern candidates with demostrated technical skills and backgrounds in computer systems, computer architecture, and/or AI. We are particularly
interested in candidates who have experience in one or more of the following areas: GPU architecture and memory systems, hardware performance characterization,
AI agents and automation, high-performance interconnects, and large-scale LLM serving.
Candidates should have experience building and evaluating systems, proficient programming skills, and familiarity with state-of-the-art research in their area
of expertise. We value candidates who are curious about how hardware systems work, enjoy designing experiments to uncover their behavior, and can translate
their findings into practical software and systems improvements. Candidates should be comfortable working in a collaborative research environment and driving
projects from initial ideas through implementation and evaluation.
Responsibilities
Research Interns put inquiry and theory into practice. Alongside fellow doctoral candidates and some of the world’s best researchers, Research Interns learn,
collaborate, and network for life. Research Interns not only advance their own careers, but they also contribute to exciting research and development strides.
During the 12-week internship, Research Interns are paired with mentors and expected to collaborate with other Research Interns and researchers, present
findings, and contribute to the vibrant life of the community. Research internships are available in all areas of research, and are offered year-round, though
they typically begin in the summer.
Responsibilities may include:
- Using AI agents to automate hardware experimentation, system management, and performance analysis.
- Reverse engineering the performance and security characteristics of modern GPU servers and their memory systems.
- Understanding and overcoming performance bottlenecks in high-bandwidth GPU interconnects, such as NVLink and NVLink-C2C.
- Improving the efficiency of LLM serving by optimizing data movement and communication across CPUs, GPUs, and memory systems.
