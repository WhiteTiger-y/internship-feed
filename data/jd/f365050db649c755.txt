Research Interns put inquiry and theory into practice. Alongside fellow doctoral candidates and some of the world's best researchers, Research Interns learn,
collaborate, and network for life. Research Interns not only advance their own careers, but they also contribute to exciting research and development strides.
During the 12-week internship, Research Interns are paired with mentors and expected to collaborate with other Research Interns and researchers, present
findings, and contribute to the vibrant life of the community. Research internships are available in all areas of research, and are offered year-round, though
they typically begin in the summer. The Research Intern will explore chemistry and physics applications for early fault-tolerant quantum computers, contributing
to the development and evaluation of algorithms and workflows that leverage emerging quantum capabilities, in combination with machine learning and/or AI.
Potential research directions include: Quantum algorithms and simulation: Develop and analyze algorithms for quantum dynamics and complex quantum systems,
including Hamiltonian simulation, and study their error and computational resource requirements. Quantum computing for chemistry and materials: Develop
computational methods, including downfolding techniques, that map complex chemical and materials problems onto efficient representations suitable for quantum
simulation. Machine learning and quantum systems: Investigate how quantum computing and machine learning can be used together to simulate complex quantum
systems, this includes exploring the limits of neural networks in capturing and learning many-body quantum complexity, to understand where quantum computers
provide a true advantage. Working closely with researchers and engineers, the Research Intern will develop new algorithmic techniques and workflows, perform
theoretical and numerical analysis, and assess their potential for scientifically meaningful applications on fault tolerant quantum computers. Currently
enrolled in a Doctorate Degree program in computer science, physics, chemistry, applied mathematics, electrical engineering, or a related STEM field. In
addition to the qualifications below, you'll need to submit a minimum of two reference letters for this position as well as a cover letter and any relevant work
or research samples. After you submit your application, a request for letters may be sent to your list of references on your behalf. Note that reference letters
cannot be requested until after you have submitted your application, and furthermore, that they might not be automatically requested for all candidates. You may
wish to alert your letter writers in advance, so they will be ready to submit your letter. Research experience in one or more of quantum algorithms, quantum
information, computational physics or chemistry, numerical methods, machine learning, or scientific computing. Experience developing and analyzing mathematical
or computational methods for scientific problems. Programming experience with a scientific computing language such as Python, C++, or Julia. Demonstrated
ability to formulate research questions, work independently, and collaborate effectively with researchers and engineers from different technical backgrounds.
